# Talk to the personas by voice

*7.69.00, behind `-c voicePersonas=true` (off by default).*

Press **Talk** on one of a project's personas and interview it out loud, in your language. It answers in its own
synthetic voice, grounded in its profile and in the real reviews of its evidence pack, and it cites the reviews it
draws on. You can interrupt it at any time. When you end the session, the transcript, a summary and key quotes are
saved as a project document you can attach to the project's work or save to the [Documents Library](documents-library.md).

The persona is synthetic. Its words are generated by AI and are never customer feedback or evidence (TENETS.md
tenet 2): the session, the transcript document and the export all say so.

Since 7.104.00 the same flag also offers **Talk it through** on a project's Product tab: a neutral AI interviewer asks
the product team about its own product, and the transcript becomes one of the tab's sources
([The product interviewer](#the-product-interviewer)).

## What a session is

- **Who:** anyone who can edit the project, acting for themselves. An MCP credential or an agent cannot start or end
  one. Only a project's own personas can talk, never the company personas.
- **Consent first:** the dialog says the transcript is kept and the audio never is, that the voice is synthetic, and
  how many of this month's minutes are left, with the estimated cost a minute. Nothing starts until the person agrees.
- **In the session:** a microphone level, live captions for both sides, the timer and the cost so far, the reviews the
  persona cites, and **End**. Speak over the persona and it stops (barge-in). A minute before the session's limit, or
  when the month's budget is nearly used, you are told; at the limit the persona says goodbye and the session ends.
- **Languages:** the persona answers in the language you speak, among the model's languages. Of the app's languages
  those are English, German, Spanish, French and Portuguese. In Japanese, Korean, Polish or Chinese the app says the
  voice does not speak your language; the persona may still answer, in English or with weaker recognition.
- **Voices:** each persona has one voice, by default `tiffany` or `matthew` (the two that speak every supported
  language), picked from a hash of the persona's id, never from its name, picture or anything that could stereotype.
  Change it on the persona's edit form (any of Nova Sonic's 16 voices, or Default). The prompt tells the model the
  voice's grammatical gender, so French, Spanish and Portuguese agree with it.

## Turning it on

```bash
npx cdk deploy --all -c voicePersonas=true
```

| Setting | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `voicePersonas` | `false` | Builds the relay runtime, the projects API's grants and the SPA's headers. Refused at synth outside the model's Regions |
| `voiceModelId` | `amazon.nova-2-5-sonic` | Nova 2.5 Sonic. `amazon.nova-2-sonic-v1:0` pins Nova 2 Sonic (end of life no sooner than 2026-12-02). Any other id, or a profile id (`us.`, `eu.`, `global.`), fails the synth |
| `voiceMonthlyMinutes` | `300` | The deployment's minutes a month (1 to 1,000,000), checked when a session starts and by the relay every minute |
| `voiceSessionMinutes` | `15` | One session's limit (1 to 15; the runtime ends a session at 20 minutes whatever it does) |
| `voiceMaxConcurrent` | `8` | Live sessions at once (1 to 20). Nova Sonic allows 20 streams per account and Region, shared with anything else in the account |

**Regions.** Nova 2 and 2.5 Sonic run in us-east-1, us-west-2, eu-north-1 and ap-northeast-1 only, in-Region (no
cross-Region profile exists), and every voice call stays in the deployment's own Region. So the flag is refused
anywhere else. **An EU deployment gets no voice in v1** (the owner's "EU in-Region only" decision): the recommended EU
Regions, eu-central-1 and eu-west-1 ([eu-deployment.md](eu-deployment.md)), have no Sonic model, and VoC cannot run in
eu-north-1, which has no Amazon Comprehend.

**Model access.** Nova Sonic needs no access request in an account where Amazon Bedrock is already enabled; check with
`aws bedrock get-foundation-model-availability --model-id amazon.nova-2-5-sonic`.

**Turning it off** removes the runtime, the grants and the headers on the next deploy. Saved transcripts stay
documents; a session row and its counters stay as they are (a few hundred bytes each).

## Limits and cost

| Limit | Value | Where |
|---|---|---|
| A session | 15 minutes (`voiceSessionMinutes`) | the relay's timer; the runtime's 20-minute lifetime above it |
| A person | 5 sessions a day, 3 starts a minute | the projects API, at the start (`VOICEDAY#`, `VOICERATE#` counters, kept 2 days) |
| The deployment | 300 minutes a month (`voiceMonthlyMinutes`) | the projects API at the start (at least one minute must be left), the relay every minute |
| The deployment's cost | 5 cents a minute of its month (`MAX_CENTS_PER_MINUTE`, shared/voice_sessions.py): $15 for 300 minutes | the projects API at the start (the month's minutes count as used up), the relay every minute (`budget`) |
| A session's cost | 5 cents a minute of its limit: 75 cents for 15 minutes | the relay, at every usage event of the model (`cost_limit` since 7.71.02, "This session reached its cost limit.": never `session_limit`, its time limit, nor `budget`, which the browser says as the month's minutes used up) |
| The microphone | 1.5 times real time (16 kHz PCM16, 48,000 bytes a second, plus a second's head start) | the relay: frames past it are dropped and counted (`dropped_frames` in its `session_ended` log line) |
| Streams at once | 8 (`voiceMaxConcurrent`) | the projects API, at the start (a live set, `VOICEUSE#LIVE`); "Voice is busy" past it |

**Cost, no fixed cost** (tenet 7). By default nothing is built. With the flag:

- Nova 2.5 Sonic, per 1,000 tokens in us-west-2: speech in $0.0031832, speech out $0.0127329, text in $0.000319, text
  out $0.002651 (the AWS Price List, read 2026-11-21). The microphone streams for the whole session (about 20 speech
  tokens a second), and the persona's speech is about 25 a second, so a conversation minute costs about 1 to 3 cents,
  typically 1.5 to 2. The consent dialog shows 2 cents a minute; the session's own cost is metered from the model's
  usage events, not from the clock. 300 minutes a month cost about $9 at most, and never more than the month's cost
  cap of $15 (the table above), whatever a session does.
- AgentCore Runtime bills each session per second while it lives (CPU is not billed while it waits on I/O): at most
  about $0.001 a minute, so at most about $0.30 for 300 minutes. Nothing runs between sessions.
- The summary is one model call over the transcript: under a cent.
- The relay's code is a ZIP in the CDK assets bucket (a few MB). No container image, no ECR repository, no VPC, NAT
  gateway or endpoint.

The cost rollup counts each session's tokens: the transcript job writes the session's `bedrock_usage` line (input =
speech + text in, output = speech + text out), so the daily rollup, which reads the `voc-*` Lambda log groups, sees
Nova Sonic beside every other model. Administration → AI shows this month's minutes, sessions, cost and live streams
(`GET /projects/voice/usage`, admins).

## How it works

```
browser ──HTTPS──▶ projects API (/projects/{proxy+})  ── checks, evidence, session row, a 60 s presigned URL
   │
   └──wss (SigV4-presigned)──▶ AgentCore Runtime endpoint ──▶ relay (one microVM per session) ──HTTP/2──▶ Nova Sonic
```

1. **The start** (`POST /projects/{id}/personas/{persona_id}/voice-sessions` `{consent: true, language}`, edit). The
   projects API checks, in this order: the flag; consent; a project persona; a person (not an MCP credential or an
   agent); a minute left this month; 3 starts this minute and 5 sessions today for this person; a free stream. Then it
   reads the persona's evidence pack as the person (the focus groups' grounding, `shared/persona_grounding.py`: at
   most 12 cited reviews and 8 fresh ones, the newest that match it (a session names no topic), each the stored text
   under its source's PII policy, screened for instructions to a model, never its author) and writes it with the
   persona's profile to
   `scratch/voice/<session>/evidence.json`, writes the session row `VOICE#<session>` / `SESSION` (status `minted`) and
   a pointer under the project, and answers a WebSocket URL presigned for **60 seconds**.
2. **The URL** is `wss://bedrock-agentcore.<region>.amazonaws.com/runtimes/<runtime ARN>/ws?…`, signed with SigV4
   query auth (service `bedrock-agentcore`) by the projects Lambda's own role, as the AgentCore SDK's
   `generate_presigned_url` does. The session id and the project scope are among the signed parameters
   (`X-Amzn-Bedrock-AgentCore-Runtime-Session-Id`, `X-Amzn-Bedrock-AgentCore-Runtime-Custom-Voc-Scope`). No AWS call
   is made to mint it. The browser holds no AWS credentials.
3. **The relay** (`voc-datalake/lambda/stream/src/voice/`, Node 22, direct code on AgentCore Runtime, PUBLIC network
   mode; its WebSocket server on `/ws` is the `ws` package) accepts the first connection for its session: it moves the row `minted → live` with a condition (the URL is
   single use), reads the evidence (which must name this session and the signed project), and opens Nova Sonic's
   bidirectional stream: the persona's prompt (its identity, a short spoken style, the voice's gender, the person's
   language, the evidence as data) and one tool, `cite`, which the persona calls silently to name the reviews it
   draws on. Barge-in is Nova Sonic's `contentEnd` with `stopReason: INTERRUPTED`; the relay tells the browser, which
   drops what it has queued.
4. **Audio:** the browser sends 16 kHz PCM16 frames of 64 ms from an AudioWorklet; the relay forwards them as the
   model's audio input and sends the persona's 24 kHz PCM16 straight back. Neither side writes audio anywhere.
5. **Long sessions:** a Nova Sonic stream lasts at most 8 minutes, so after 7 minutes, at the persona's next finished
   turn (at 7:45 whatever it is doing), the relay opens a new stream with the same prompt and the conversation so far
   as its chat history (at most 200 KB, oldest turns dropped first), moves the microphone to it and closes the old
   one. The meter sums every stream.
6. **The meter:** every minute and at the end the relay adds the session's seconds and cost since the last write to
   the month's row, whose new total is the budget check, then writes its seconds, tokens and cost to the session's row
   (the month first: a session whose row is gone still counts its time and cost to the month).
7. **The end:** End sends `{type: 'end'}`; the relay closes the stream, writes the last transcript part and the usage,
   marks the row `ended` and answers `ended`; then the browser calls `POST …/voice-sessions/{session}/end`, which stops
   the runtime session if it still runs and starts the transcript job. The browser retries that call (three attempts,
   then **Try again**), and sends it once more with `keepalive` when the page goes away after the session ended but
   before its End was recorded. A page that goes away while the session is still live sends no End (since 7.71.01):
   its WebSocket closes, and the relay finishes on its own (the last turns, the meter, the row marked ended), which an
   End stopping the runtime could cut short mid-write. A session whose End never arrived is saved the next time its
   starter reads it (`GET …/voice-sessions/{session}`) or opens the Talk dialog of any persona of its project (the
   offer saves the starter's five newest sessions there that ended without End, through the project's pointers); a URL
   never used ends at the first read 70 seconds after it was made (its row's `connect_by`: its minute and 10 seconds
   for the clocks, below). At the time limit, the session's cost cap or the budget the persona's goodbye, already
   sent, plays out (at most 10 seconds) before the browser releases the audio.
8. **On the wire,** every end of a connection, a refusal or a session's, is `ended {reason}` then the close code, in
   that order (`lambda/stream/src/voice/wire-end.ts`, 7.71.02): nothing goes before the connection is a second old, then
   `ws` writes the two frames in that order, and destroys a socket whose close handshake has not finished five seconds
   after the close (its `closeTimeout`, `server.ts`; 7.71.03, in place of the relay's own two timers). On the b-
   deployment a refusal sent right after the upgrade never reached the browser (no `ended`, no 1008: AgentCore's proxy
   forwarded neither, `ws` waited its default 30-second close timeout and the browser saw 1006), while a first
   connection's `ready`, a few hundred milliseconds after its upgrade, always arrived. **That AgentCore's proxy now
   forwards a refusal so ordered is not verified: check it on a live deployment
   ([What to check](#what-to-check-on-a-live-deployment), step 1).**

**Routes** (all on `/projects/{proxy+}`; no API Gateway resource was added):

| Method | Path | Who | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| GET | `/projects/{id}/personas/{persona_id}/voice-sessions` | view | The offer: `{enabled, voice_id, voice_gender, languages, session_minutes, minutes_left, sessions_left_today, estimated_cents_per_minute}` |
| POST | `/projects/{id}/personas/{persona_id}/voice-sessions` | edit, a person | 201 `{session_id, connect_url, expires_in: 60, …}`; 400 `consent_required`, 404 `voice_off`, 409 `budget_exhausted`, 429 `rate_limited` / `daily_limit` / `busy`, 403 `person_only` |
| GET | `/projects/{id}/personas/{persona_id}/voice-sessions/{session_id}` | view | `{status, seconds, cost_cents, end_reason, transcript_document_id, job_id, …}` |
| POST | `/projects/{id}/personas/{persona_id}/voice-sessions/{session_id}/end` | edit, its starter or an admin | 202: stopped, ended, the transcript job started |
| GET | `/projects/voice/usage` | admins acting as themselves | The month's minutes, sessions, cost and live streams |
| GET, POST | `/projects/{id}/product-interview/voice-sessions`, `…/{session_id}`, `…/{session_id}/end` | as the persona's four | The product interviewer's offer, start, state and End ([below](#the-product-interviewer)) |

## The transcript

The transcript job (`assist_voice_finalize`, the projects assist twin, `shared/voice_transcripts.py`) files one
`persona_interview` document in the project:

- `synthetic: true`, and its content opens with "Synthetic voice interview: the persona's words are generated by AI…".
- The person's turns go through the PII redaction (`shared/pii_redaction.py`: e-mail addresses, phone and card
  numbers, IBANs and the like); the persona's turns as it spoke them, each followed by the reviews it cited as
  `[fb:<id>]`. A cited id that is not in the persona's evidence pack is dropped. A persona's turn is its FINAL text
  (what was said, which follows playback), then the SPECULATIVE sentences that text has not covered yet: a turn's last
  sentence may never get its FINAL text before the turn closes or the session ends (7.71.02). A turn the person spoke
  over keeps only its FINAL text.
- A summary and up to 5 key quotes from one model call. A key quote is kept only when it is in the persona's words
  word for word; if the call fails, the transcript is saved without them.
- Its lineage is the cited reviews only (`input_feedback_ids`); an erasure removes them as it does a focus group's.
  The methodology never counts it: it is a reference, never evidence.
- D4: it records its creator (the session's starter), when, and `derivation` (the session, the persona, the model and
  voice, how long, how many reviews were cited).

## The product interviewer

*7.104.00, the same `-c voicePersonas=true`.* The Product tab's gaps panel ([project-workspace.md → Product
tab](project-workspace.md#product-tab)) offers **Talk it through** beside typing the answers. It is the persona
session with another subject (`shared/voice_subjects.py`): the same consent dialog, relay, model, meter, renewal, wire
end and limits, and the same counters. A product interview counts against the person's 5 sessions a day and 3 starts
a minute, the deployment's month and its streams, as a persona session does.

- **Routes**, on `/projects/{id}/product-interview/voice-sessions`: GET the offer (view), POST a start (edit, a person,
  `{consent: true, language?, questions?}`), GET `…/{session_id}` its state (view), POST `…/{session_id}/end` (its
  starter or an admin). The answers have the persona routes' shapes; a session's state also says `kind:
  product_interview`, and `source_doc_id` once it is saved. The checks run in the persona start's order (the flag,
  a person, consent, the language, the questions, a project and not the company library, then room for one more
  source, the month's minutes, the minute's and the day's starts, a free stream). Its transcript becomes one of the
  project's sources, so a project that already holds its 20 is refused at the start (400 `too_many_sources`: "Delete
  a source first", before any counter moves), and the offer says so first (`sources_full: true`; the consent dialog
  shows that sentence instead of Start): an interview is never held that could not be saved. A session is answered only through the routes of its own subject:
  a persona session's id is a 404 on the product routes and the reverse. The start's consent says the transcript is
  kept, the audio never is, and the interviewer is an AI.
- **The questions:** the start's `questions` (`[{field, question}]`, at most 12, each naming a product field and at
  most 200 characters: the gaps panel sends the ones it shows, an extract's or the defaults; a field named twice is
  asked once, as first named, so a proposal that holds two questions for a field still starts), else one for every
  empty field of the product context (every field when none is empty). An empty question asks the field's default,
  the chat interview's (`lambda/stream/src/assistant/tools/product-interview.ts`). The evidence file at the same
  scratch key holds `{kind: 'product_interview', project_name, language, questions, known}` instead of a persona and
  its pack: `known` is what the form already says, each value cut at 500 characters. The relay's role is unchanged.
- **The voice:** the default pair, picked from a hash of the project's id.
- **The prompt** (`interviewerPrompt`, `lambda/stream/src/voice/prompt.ts`): a neutral AI interviewer, not a customer
  or a persona, and it says it is an AI when asked. It asks the questions one at a time in their order, keeps its
  turns short, confirms what it heard, asks once to clarify, moves on when the person does not know or skips, and
  never invents, suggests or completes an answer. It speaks the person's language (the UI's when it cannot tell). The
  project's name, the questions and the known values reach it as data in a `<context>` block, each one JSON string
  on its own line, so a value can neither close the block nor start a line of its own. It has no tool: the stream
  opens with no tool configuration.
- **What is saved:** no summary call and no document. The transcript job writes the turns as Markdown, the
  interviewer's labelled "AI interviewer" and the person's "Team member", every turn through the same PII redaction
  (the person's, and the interviewer's too: it confirms what it heard, so it repeats the person's words), and saves
  it as one `interview` source of the project (`interview_mode: voice`, its `session_id`) through
  `shared/product_sources.py` `save_interview_source`, the one way answers become a source. The session row keeps the
  source's id (`source_doc_id`); the scratch files go as a persona session's do. A session nobody spoke in saves
  nothing. A project that filled its 20 sources during the interview (sources added while it ran) still gets it, past
  the cap, rather than lose it: the row and the session's state say `over_source_cap: true`, the end of the session
  says the project now holds more than 20 and to delete one before adding another, and the next add is refused until
  one is deleted. Any other refusal marks the session `failed`. The source is the team's own description of its
  product, never customer evidence; the next Extract reads it.

## Data and retention

| What | Where | Kept |
|---|---|---|
| The session row (status, meter, ids; never text) | voc-projects `VOICE#<session>` / `SESSION` | with its project: a project delete removes it |
| The project's pointer to it | voc-projects `PROJECT#<id>` / `VOICE#<session>` | with its project |
| The evidence (profile and review quotes) | raw bucket `scratch/voice/<session>/evidence.json` | deleted (every version) when the transcript is saved; `scratch/` expires after 2 days in any case |
| The transcript parts (the person's words, not yet redacted) | raw bucket `scratch/voice/<session>/transcript/part-NNNN.json`, written once each | as the evidence |
| The month's usage, the live set | voc-aggregates `VOICEUSE#DEPLOYMENT#<yyyy-mm>`, `VOICEUSE#LIVE` | the month's row 400 days |
| A person's day and minute | voc-aggregates `VOICEDAY#<sub>#<date>`, `VOICERATE#<sub>#<minute>` | 2 days |
| The transcript document | voc-projects, a project document | as every document |
| A product interview's transcript (7.104.00) | voc-projects, a product source (`PRODUCT_DOC#<id>`, `interview`) | as every product source |
| **Audio** | **nowhere** | **never stored** |

Projects are the deployment's until phase 3 of [multi-workspace](multi-workspace.md), so a session's keys are the
deployment's too (no `WS#` variant yet), and a workspace's own monthly budget comes with them. The workspace export
leaves a session's row out (its state and meter, not the project's content) and exports the transcript as a document.

**Bedrock model invocation logging** is the account's, not VoC's. If an operator turned it on for the account (Text
modality, with large-data delivery to S3), it might capture the stream's JSON events, which carry base64 audio. It is
not known whether bidirectional streams are logged at all. Check `aws bedrock get-model-invocation-logging-configuration`
before turning voice on in an account that logs invocations.

## Security review

The relay is a new entry point: AgentCore Runtime's public endpoint in front of our code. This is the review the plan
asked for (`docs/voice-personas.md` is its record; the S-15 spike's findings are its source).

**Authentication.** The runtime is configured for SigV4 (IAM) inbound auth only: no JWT authorizer, so a Cognito
token cannot reach it, and AgentCore refuses a request without a valid SigV4 signature before our container sees it.
The only signatures a browser ever holds are the projects API's presigned URLs:

- valid 60 seconds (`X-Amz-Expires=60`);
- for one GET to one runtime (its ARN is in the signed path) with one session id and one project scope (both signed
  query parameters, so neither can be changed without breaking the signature);
- they carry the projects Lambda role's session token, never its secret key, and the role's AgentCore grant is only
  `InvokeAgentRuntimeWithWebSocketStream` and `StopRuntimeSession` on that runtime and its default endpoint.

**Authorization** stays in the projects API, before any URL exists: edit access to the project (its middleware), a
person acting for themselves, consent, the budget and the limits. The relay then holds the session to it:

- **single use:** it activates the row `minted → live` with a condition (its `connect_by` not passed: the mint's time
  plus the URL's 60 seconds and 10 seconds for the minting Lambda's clock and the relay's, `shared/voice_sessions.py`
  `CONNECT_SKEW_SECONDS`); a second connection, or one after the session ended, is refused (`refused`) with close code
  1008;
- **its own expiry:** AgentCore accepted a URL 65.8 seconds after it was signed with `X-Amz-Expires=60` (the b-
  deployment, 2026-11-23: a grace of its own, not documented). The relay does not rely on it: a first connection more
  than 70 seconds after the mint fails that condition and is refused like a used one (`refused`, 1008). The projects
  API ends such a row (`disconnected`, its place in the live set freed) at the first read once the same `connect_by`
  has passed, never sooner. The row holds the one deadline, written by the mint and read as written by both, so
  neither side keeps a skew of its own (since 7.71.04; 7.71.03 kept the 10 seconds in both languages, pinned alike by a
  test, and 7.71.02 ended such a connection `expired`, a reason of its own, which 7.71.03 removed);
- **bound to its project:** the evidence it reads must name its session id and the signed project scope, else 1008;
- **bound to its pointer:** a session is answered, ended and saved only through its project's own pointer row, which
  the relay cannot write, so a session row cannot be moved to another project.

**The relay's role** (`lib/stacks/voice-relay.ts`, pinned statement by statement in `lib/stacks/voice-relay.test.ts`):

- `bedrock:InvokeModel` on the one Nova Sonic model ARN in the deployment's Region (the action that authorizes
  `InvokeModelWithBidirectionalStream`; there is no other): no other model, profile or `InvokeModelWithResponseStream`;
- `dynamodb:UpdateItem` on voc-projects held by `dynamodb:LeadingKeys` to `VOICE#*` and by `dynamodb:Attributes` to the
  session row's status and meter; on voc-aggregates to `VOICEUSE#*` and the month's and live set's attributes; no
  read, put, query, scan or delete, and never a `PROJECT#` row;
- `s3:GetObject` on `scratch/voice/*/evidence.json` and `s3:PutObject` on `scratch/voice/*/transcript/*` only; no list,
  no delete;
- `kms:Decrypt` and `kms:GenerateDataKey` on the stack's key, only through DynamoDB and S3; its runtime's own log groups;
- nothing on the feedback table, SSM, Secrets Manager, Lambda, ECR, or `bedrock-agentcore:*`. Its trust is
  `bedrock-agentcore.amazonaws.com` for this account's AgentCore resources in this Region.

A compromised relay could therefore misstate its own session's meter or transcript (a transcript is the persona's and
the person's words, treated as data: redacted, bounded, its citations kept to the pack, quotes checked word for word)
and read other sessions' evidence (review quotes the person who started each one could already see), and nothing else.

**The container** listens on `0.0.0.0:8080` (AgentCore's proxy reaches it over the microVM's interface): in PUBLIC
mode it is reachable only through AgentCore's endpoint, never directly. It answers AgentCore's HTTP contract:
`GET /ping` (`Healthy`, or `HealthyBusy` while a conversation is live, dated when that status last changed, so the
runtime's 2-minute idle timeout ends a microVM whose conversation is over), `POST /invocations` (400: the relay talks
over its WebSocket only) and `/ws`. `/ws` is served by the [`ws`](https://github.com/websockets/ws) package
(8.23.0, MIT, no dependencies; approved by the owner on 2026-11-22, [dependencies.md](dependencies.md)): the
handshake, the framing, the close handshake and the answer to a WebSocket ping are ws's, and the relay holds no
hand-written RFC 6455 code; the dev mock (`frontend/mock-voice.js`, development only) keeps its own framing. It takes messages of at most 32 KB (close 1009), no compression (`perMessageDeflate`
off), and only valid close codes leave it. ws's optional native add-ons (`bufferutil`, `utf-8-validate`) are left out
of `relay.js`. The relay logs ids and counts only (never audio, transcript text, the prompt or the evidence), and
holds the microphone to 1.5 times real time (frames past it are dropped).

**The browser.** With the flag, the SPA's response headers allow `microphone=(self)` (only its own origin, never the
public widget, which keeps `microphone=()`) and add `wss://bedrock-agentcore.<region>.amazonaws.com` to `connect-src`.
Without the flag both are exactly as before. The capture worklet is a same-origin file (`script-src 'self'`).

**Model safety.** Nova Sonic supports neither Guardrails nor abuse detection. Safety rests on the persona's prompt
(evidence as data, a short spoken style), the evidence-only `cite` tool, the transcript's PII redaction and the
synthetic labels. Every generated audio carries the model's inaudible watermark.

**Abuse and spend** are bounded by the limits above: the month's minutes and its cost cap (checked at the start and
every minute, with a hard stop), each session's cost cap (checked at every usage event), the microphone held to 1.5
times real time (so a browser that sends faster than it speaks can neither grow the model stream's queue nor its
cost), the per-person day and minute, the concurrent streams, and the 15-minute session. A session whose project is
deleted while it runs ends (`error`) at its next write: the relay's updates of its row hold only while the row exists,
so they never make it again.

**The refusals the tests pin** (shared/test/test_voice_sessions.py, the relay's specs, lib/stacks/voice-relay.test.ts):
no consent; an MCP credential or an agent; a company persona; the budget used, in minutes or in cost; a fourth start
in a minute; a sixth session in a day; a busy deployment; a URL whose session id or scope was changed (a different
signature); a second connection to a live session (1008); an activation that fails its condition (1008); a first
connection more than 70 seconds after the mint (`refused`, 1008); evidence naming another session or project (1008); a
message over 32 KB, in one frame or in fragments (1009); a microphone faster than real time (dropped); a session past
its cost cap (`cost_limit`); a deleted session row (`error`, its time and cost still added to the month); an end's
order on the wire (`ended`, then the close, not before the connection is a second old: `wire-end.test.ts`); a close
handshake the peer never answers, ended five seconds after the close (`server.test.ts`); the flag
refused outside the four Regions, for any other model id or a profile id. `lib/stacks/voice-relay.test.ts` also runs
the built `relay.js` and checks that it answers `/ping` and a `/ws` handshake.

**Not verified without a deployment** (see below): that AgentCore refuses an expired URL, an edited query and another
deployment's runtime ARN; that an established connection outlives the URL's 60 seconds; that the signed custom header
reaches the container.

## What to check on a live deployment

Deploy to a test account first (us-west-2), then:

1. **The refusals:** a URL used after 60 seconds, a URL with an edited session id or scope, a second browser tab on
   the same URL, and a URL for another deployment's runtime are all refused by AgentCore or the relay. A refusal by
   the relay reaches the browser as `ended {refused}` and close 1008 about a second after it opened, never a 1006
   thirty seconds later (7.71.02's order on the wire, not yet seen through AgentCore's proxy; were the close frame
   still lost, the connection would end about five seconds after the refusal, 7.71.03).
2. **The connection outlives the URL:** a session goes past its first minute.
3. **The scope header** reaches the container (`X-Amzn-Bedrock-AgentCore-Runtime-Custom-Voc-Scope`): a session starts
   at all (the relay refuses one without it).
4. **The runtime starts:** CloudFormation creates the direct-code runtime from the CDK asset (`NODE_22`,
   `relay.js`), its role is assumed, and `/ping` answers.
5. **Latency:** microphone to first audio, end of turn to first audio (the spike measured about 0.4–0.9 s after the
   model's end of turn, plus the 1.75 s endpointing pause), and a new session's cold start.
6. **Barge-in and captions:** speaking over the persona stops it at once; the persona's FINAL captions arrive (the
   spike saw only SPECULATIVE text before it closed), and a FINAL caption and the saved transcript keep every sentence
   the persona planned, even one whose own FINAL text never came (7.71.02: on the b- deployment both kept only the
   first of two).
7. **A 15-minute session** rolls over at about 7 minutes without a noticeable gap, and its meter counts both streams.
8. **Tokens per minute** against the estimate, from the session row's `tokens` and the `bedrock_usage` line.
9. **The budget stop:** with `-c voiceMonthlyMinutes=1`, the persona says goodbye and the session ends.
10. **The idle end:** after a conversation ends, its runtime session ends about 2 minutes later (the idle timeout:
    `/ping` dates its status by its last change), not at the 20-minute `maxLifetime`.
11. **The product interviewer** (7.104.00): on the Product tab, **Talk it through** asks the gaps panel's questions one
    at a time, in English and in one more supported language, says it is an AI when asked, and invents no answer; at
    End the transcript appears as an `interview` source (voice) of the tab, every turn redacted, and the
    session counts in Administration → AI. A persona session's id is a 404 on the product routes. The stream opens
    with no tool configuration: check that Nova Sonic accepts a `promptStart` without one.
12. **A closed tab:** closing the page mid-session lets the relay finish the session (its row `ended`, the last turns
    written), and the transcript is filed when its starter next opens Talk in the project (or reads the session);
    closing it after the session ended but before its End was recorded files it through the `keepalive` End.
